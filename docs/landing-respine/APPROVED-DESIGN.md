## Problem Statement

sendlyr.com (live since 2026-07-21, v1.1.0.0) describes a company Sendlyr is not, in a
category it cannot win, selling an offer that was killed, with proof numbers that no longer
match the database.

Four specific failures:

1. **Category collision.** The homepage centerpiece is titled "The continuous decision layer"
   with the subhead "One decision that gets better with every measured outcome," and
   `/#decision-layer` is a permanent nav item. That is Aampe's product description. Braze
   bought OfferFit for $325M (completed Jun 2025); MoEngage bought Aampe (announced
   2026-06-24). A Braze employee reads that headline and hears "small OfferFit" in three
   seconds. Braze City x City Sydney is 2026-08-18.
2. **Wrong output shape.** The decisions the page shows leaving the system are *message*
   decisions: "Return to the unfinished task with one clear next step," "One reminder:
   subject, body, and deep link," a SendGrid mark. Deciding which message to send is the
   market with $325M and MoEngage's balance sheet in it.
3. **Stale proof.** `/blog/pai-discovery-case-study` publishes a 178-user mid-experiment cut
   (121 treatment / 57 control, 69.4% vs 57.9%, "19.9% relative lift and 11.5-point
   absolute"). The 2026-08-04 re-pull from Supabase `experiment_outcomes` gives different
   figures across 283 users. `public/llms.txt` carries the stale number too, machine-readable.
4. **Dead offer, no measurement.** `/how-it-works` sells "The Activation Signal Sprint,"
   killed 2026-08-08. And no analytics has ever run on production: `ANALYTICS_ENABLED` is
   false, and the PostHog logo in the evidence diagram is a decorative SVG, not an install.
   Nothing about this page has ever been measured.

**The deeper problem, found during the session.** The proposed fix was to re-spine the page
around *finding* the behavior that predicts conversion. Sendlyr's own only experiment is a
counterexample to that claim. Inside the control arm — clean, unexposed, no post-treatment
contamination — hitting the indicator did **not** predict conversion: 75.5% (n=49) for
hitters vs 78.4% (n=37) for non-hitters. A behavior selected because it correlated with
conversion showed no relationship under randomisation. Leading with "we find the behavior
that predicts conversion" puts the headline and the case study in direct contradiction, and
a sharp reader will use one against the other.

## Status Quo

A lifecycle or growth owner at a subscription app picks an activation behavior from a
correlation — a dashboard cross-tab, a "users who do X convert 2x" slide, or convention —
then builds onboarding and lifecycle campaigns to drive it. The correlation is never tested
against a holdout, because building the holdout requires an analyst, a randomiser, and a
pre-registered outcome that nobody owns. So the team scales an intervention toward a
behavior that may not cause anything, and the campaign result cannot distinguish "the
behavior mattered" from "the users who were going to convert did the behavior anyway."

Sendlyr has direct evidence this failure is real, from its own experiment.

## Target User & Narrowest Wedge

**User.** A lifecycle, retention, or growth owner at an SMB or mid-market subscription /
product-led company that **already has a lifecycle team and stack**. Sendlyr does not replace
them; it feeds them a validated signal and a readout, and they execute in Braze, Customer.io,
Iterable, or whatever they run. Moved up-market deliberately on 2026-08-08 (SMB/mid-market
decides fast; enterprise runs 12+ month cycles).

**Structural qualifier, which is also the moat.** Aampe requires an existing CDP plus CPaaS.
OfferFit requires a Braze-scale contract. Everyone below that infrastructure floor is
unserved. Hypothesis plus a real holdout works at roughly 500 eligible users; per-user
reinforcement learning does not, because it needs event density.

**Wedge.** One candidate behavior, one holdout, one readout. Not discovery of the behavior —
validation of it. The customer brings the theory; Sendlyr finds out if it is true.

### Hero (validation spine — primary draft)

> **Your team already has a theory about what makes users convert. Sendlyr finds out if it's true.**
>
> We take one candidate behavior, design a real holdout, and tell you whether driving it moved
> the outcome — including when it didn't. You keep Braze, Customer.io, or Iterable. We tell

Eyebrow candidate, also the OG line: *"Most activation metrics are correlations. We run the holdout."*

### Add
- **Qualifier block.** "Built for experiments starting around 500 eligible users. No CDP,
  CPaaS, or per-user reinforcement-learning system required. Power still depends on your
  baseline rate and the effect size you care about."
- **Three-part deliverable.** Signal analysis → controlled experiment design → readout.
- **"What you leave with"**, ending at the readout, not at a measurement plan: signal lift and
  coverage, state distribution, sends, indicator by arm, outcome by arm, stated uncertainty,
  and a go / stop / iterate conclusion. This is the attribution defence — signal alone can be
  run in Braze without Sendlyr getting credit for the lift, and the lift is the proof asset.
- **Real-results section: "We moved the behavior. Conversion did not move."** Corrected
  figures, both the win and the null, plus the control-arm finding.
- **Founder-evidence block.** Stephen designed and operated a live randomised experiment across
  485 users and 568 hand-executed sends, detected a four-day pipeline outage himself, and
  corrected the published conclusion when the final data contradicted the hypothesis.
- **Signal Validation Pilot**, fixed scope, priced at AUD $2,500, behind a qualification form:
  cohort size, lifecycle stack, target outcome, timeline, budget authority.
- **First-party analytics**: source, CTA click, case-study visit, form start, form submit.

### Correct
`/blog/pai-discovery-case-study` and `public/llms.txt` to the 2026-08-04 Supabase pull:

| | Treatment | Control |
|---|---|---|
| Indicator (≥1 course in first 4 days) | 69.4% (129/186) | 57.7% (56/97) |
| Trial conversion | 76.8% (136/177) | 76.7% (66/86) |

Indicator: +11.7pp, +20.3% relative, z ≈ 1.95, **p ≈ 0.051 — borderline, not clean
significance.** Conversion: +0.1pp, no lift. Ceiling effect (~77% baseline) is the leading
explanation. Control-arm cross-tab: indicator hitters 75.5% (n=49) vs non-hitters 78.4%
(n=37); CI roughly −21pp to +15pp, so a moderate effect cannot be ruled out. Do not report the
treatment/no-hit subgroup — `pai_hit` is post-treatment and conditioning on it breaks
randomisation.
