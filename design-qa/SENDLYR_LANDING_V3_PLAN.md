## Problem Statement

The current homepage leads with one Typesy experiment. It proves a result, but it does not explain why a team needs Sendlyr.

Sendlyr is also framed too narrowly around lifecycle execution. The stronger problem is shared by Product and Lifecycle teams: product behavior changes quickly, candidate signals move, and old conclusions become stale.

The page must explain a repeatable system:

1. Find the behaviors that predict progress.
2. Rank them using descriptive lift, coverage, and reviewed data quality.
3. Track when those rankings change.
4. Design and monitor tests across product UX and lifecycle.
5. Turn validated signals into the next product decision.

## Demand Evidence

Sophiie already has PostHog, ClickHouse, and Hightouch. Their problem is not missing data.

The meeting surfaced four concrete needs:

- Product and Lifecycle teams need actionable findings from the same usage data.
- A fast-changing product makes one-off analysis decay quickly.
- Teams want a ranked list of leading-indicator hypotheses over time.
- Teams want test recommendations and experiment monitoring across product and lifecycle changes.

The dashboard drew the strongest interest. Its value is continuity: a team can see what leads today, what changed, and what should be tested next.

Typesy VIP remains supporting evidence. Its first-course experiment observed a 69.4% treatment rate versus 57.9% control.

## Status Quo

Teams already collect product events in analytics tools and warehouses. Analysts pull one-off reports, product managers choose plausible onboarding metrics, and lifecycle teams build campaigns around those guesses.

The workflow breaks when the product changes:

- Event definitions drift.
- New features introduce new candidate behaviors.
- Previously strong indicators weaken.
- Product and Lifecycle run separate tests.
- Nobody owns the current signal ranking.

The current Sendlyr page mirrors this one-off workflow. It shows one result instead of the continuing decision system.

## Target User & Narrowest Wedge

The primary buyer is a Product leader at a fast-changing subscription or usage-based app with an existing event warehouse.

Growth and Lifecycle leaders are collaborators. The analyst closest to the data and the security owner approve the first scoped data sample.

The narrowest wedge remains the Activation Signal Sprint:

- Scope the safest approved data sample.
- Rank candidate activation signals.
- Define the first validation test.
- Deliver a signal brief, first test design, and monitoring specification.

The Sprint is the entry offer. A future monitoring product explains the recurring direction without being promised as a finished deliverable.

## Constraints

- Keep the public offer as the Activation Signal Sprint.
- Do not present Sendlyr as an email-only or lifecycle-only tool.
- Use “activation signal” and “leading indicator” in public copy.
- Use “PAI” only in optional technical detail, if at all.
- Treat Product and Lifecycle interventions as equal test surfaces.
- Lead with the customer problem, not the Typesy result.
- Keep Typesy claims descriptive and preserve the published evidence boundary.
- Balance concise copy with product media, interaction, and explanatory depth.
- Do not fabricate a live customer dashboard or unsupported integrations.
- Label the website dashboard exactly: `Interactive concept preview using illustrative data. Not a live customer account.`
- Distinguish the website concept preview from the client Sprint deliverable.
- Preserve the static, dependency-light architecture and current design system.

## Premises

1. The strongest repeatable pain is stale activation knowledge, not insufficient data.
2. The Sprint can remain the commercial entry point while monitoring demonstrates recurring value.
3. Product UX and lifecycle messages are both interventions against the same leading indicators.
4. Daily snapshots are a plausible pilot input, not a public integration promise.
5. The Typesy result should validate the method after visitors understand why they need it.
6. Media should depict the product’s decision model, not generic analytics dashboards.

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: Improved Sprint website

Keep the Activation Signal Sprint as the hero offer. Expand the story from one-off discovery to a monitoring-ready deliverable and product direction.

**Effort:** Medium across four delivery phases  
**Risk:** Medium until preview fields and public claims are approved

Advantages:

- Matches the current commercial offer.
- Can be implemented honestly with product-preview media.
- Makes Sophiie’s dashboard interest visible without claiming a finished platform.

Tradeoffs:

- Sendlyr can still appear service-led.
- Recurring product value must be shown visually, not left to explanatory copy.

### Approach B: Continuous signal dashboard

Lead with the dashboard as the product. Present the Sprint as onboarding and pilot setup.

**Effort:** Large  
**Risk:** Medium

Advantages:

- Closest match to the strongest Sophiie interest.
- Establishes recurring software value immediately.

Tradeoffs:

- Risks presenting a more mature product than exists today.
- Requires stronger product captures and clearer operating details.

### Approach C: Product decision platform

Present Sendlyr as the full decision layer between warehouse data, teams, experiments, and execution.

**Effort:** Extra large  
**Risk:** High

Advantages:

- Communicates the complete long-term vision.
- Gives Product and Lifecycle equal prominence.

Tradeoffs:

- Broad language can become generic.
- The page could promise integrations, automation, or maturity that cannot yet be demonstrated.

## Recommended Approach

Proceed with Approach A, as selected by the user.

The homepage should sell a Sprint with a continuing payoff:

> Find the signals that matter now. Define how to keep them current.

The Sprint discovers and validates the first activation signal. The website concept preview explains the intended monitoring product without presenting it as a live customer dashboard.

### Narrative hierarchy

1. **Why now:** Product changes faster than one-off analysis.
2. **What Sendlyr does:** Finds and ranks activation signals, then defines how to monitor them.
3. **Who uses it:** Product, Growth, and Lifecycle teams.
4. **What teams see:** Ranking movement, evidence, and test status.
5. **How work happens:** Scope, rank, test, plan monitoring.
6. **Why believe it:** Typesy VIP supporting result.
7. **How to start:** Book an Activation Signal Sprint.

### Page-flow constraint

If the first screen can communicate only three things, they are:

1. **Need:** product changes make activation knowledge go stale.
2. **Product idea:** a ranked signal preview shows what matters now and what moved.
3. **Action:** the Activation Signal Sprint finds the first signal and defines the next test.

The first viewport cannot add experiment statistics, vertical examples, extra offers, or integration logos.

### User journey storyboard

The approved emotional sequence is product demonstration first. The concept preview appears in the first composition, while the need and offer remain readable without interacting.

| Horizon | User does | Intended feeling | Design response |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| First 5 seconds | Scans the headline and ranking preview | `I can see what this product helps decide.` | One selected signal, visible movement, one proposed test state, and a clear Sprint CTA |
| First 30 seconds | Selects a signal or changes Product/Lifecycle view | `This connects product behavior to an actual test.` | Prior rank, recent observation, and test sequence update together |
| First 2 minutes | Reads why rankings move and how the Sprint works | `This is more disciplined than another analytics dashboard.` | Non-causal release context, data-sample scope, one changed surface, and monitoring-plan ownership |

The page should not require analytics knowledge. Plain-language definitions appear before lift and coverage details.

### Homepage structure

#### 1. Hero: keep activation knowledge current

The first screen must state the need before the method.

Proposed message:

- Eyebrow: `Activation signals for changing products`
- H1: `Know what drives progress now.`
- Supporting copy: `Sendlyr finds and ranks leading indicators, then designs product and lifecycle tests with a monitoring plan.`
- Primary action: `Book an Activation Signal Sprint`
- Secondary action: `Explore the signal dashboard`

The secondary action anchors to `#signal-dashboard`, moves focus to its heading after scrolling, and uses instant movement for reduced-motion users.

The hero visual is a product-preview dashboard, not the Typesy cohort. It always carries the full illustrative-data label.

#### 2. Problem: yesterday’s signal can mislead today

Show the gap between existing data infrastructure and current decisions.

Three short observations:

- Your product changes.
- User behavior changes with it.
- Your activation model must keep up.

Use a visual timeline showing a ranked signal moving near a product release. The marker provides timing context and does not claim causation.

#### 3. Shared decision layer

Explain that Sendlyr is not limited to email.

Use one activation signal feeding two coordinated test lanes:

- Product: onboarding guidance and in-product prompts.
- Lifecycle: email, push, and audience timing.

These are initial Sprint test surfaces, not a promise to optimize every product or pricing decision. The interaction lets visitors switch between Product, Lifecycle, and Combined views without hiding core content from non-JavaScript users.

#### 4. Signal dashboard preview

This is the primary interactive media section.

Show in semantic HTML:

- Ranked candidate signals.
- Descriptive lift and coverage columns.
- Movement since the previous snapshot.
- A product-release marker that coincides with a ranking change.
- Current test status for the selected signal.

Visitors can select one signal and inspect the observations associated with its rank change. The page must not claim it knows the cause.

Definitions remain visible beside the table:

- **Descriptive lift:** the relative difference in a later outcome between users who did and did not show the early behavior. It is not causal proof.
- **Coverage:** the share of eligible new users who showed the behavior.
- **Data quality review:** a human review of event definitions, cohort completeness, sample size, and stability. It is described beside the table, not reduced to a public score.

##### Canonical illustrative fixture

Visible label: `Interactive concept preview using illustrative data. Not a live customer account.`

| ---: | --- | ---: | ---: | --- | --- |
| 1 | Complete a first meaningful task within two days | +18% | 44% | Up two | Onboarding prompt · Draft |
| 2 | Return for a second active session within four days | +15% | 61% | Down one | No test |
| 3 | Use a core feature three times within seven days | +12% | 28% | Down one | Reminder · Running |

The previous snapshot ranks second session first, repeated core-feature use second, and first meaningful task third.

The timeline marker reads `Onboarding release · 14 May`. Adjacent copy says: `Timing context only. This preview does not attribute the ranking change to the release.`

Selecting a row updates three visible fields: prior rank, recent observation, and proposed next test. The first row is the semantic default without JavaScript.

The Product tab displays the Product test. The Lifecycle tab displays the Lifecycle test. Combined displays the sequencing rule, keeping one changed surface at a time.

#### 5. Sprint workflow

Use four stages instead of the current lifecycle-heavy three:

1. Scope the safest approved data sample.
2. Rank activation signals.
3. Design the first test.
4. Define the monitoring plan.

Each stage answers one practical concern:

1. What data sample is needed, excluded, and approved?
2. How are descriptive lift, coverage, and data-quality review used?
3. What changes in Product or Lifecycle, and what remains control?
4. Who would review ranking changes, and on what cadence?

#### 6. Supporting proof: Typesy VIP

Move the experiment below the product story.

Keep the exact public result together:

- Treatment: 69.4%, 84 of 121 users.
- Control: 57.9%, 33 of 57 users.
- Relative lift: +19.9%.
- Absolute difference: +11.5 percentage points.

Present it as proof that a leading indicator can become a controlled test. Keep this exact caveat adjacent:

> This experiment observed a leading-indicator lift. This public summary does not establish statistical significance or long-term retention lift.

The source of truth remains `/blog/pai-discovery-case-study`. The homepage cannot add causal, significance, or retention claims beyond that page.

#### 7. Deliverable and CTA

The current public Sprint deliverable is:

- A ranked activation-signal brief.
- A first experiment design.
- A monitoring specification with proposed cadence and ownership.
- A shared Product and Lifecycle action plan.

The interactive website dashboard is a concept preview. A client-facing monitoring dashboard is pilot or roadmap scope until its refresh cadence, access model, and hosting are confirmed.

Close with the same Sprint CTA. Do not introduce a second offer.

### Media plan

The page requires four visual compositions:

1. **Hero dashboard:** semantic HTML/CSS/JS using the canonical fixture.
2. **Ranking-shift visual:** semantic HTML timeline with an optional decorative raster layer.
3. **Shared test map:** semantic HTML with an optional supporting illustration.
4. **Typesy evidence visual:** semantic treatment/control proof lower on the page.

Decision-critical values remain HTML. Raster assets are decorative or supporting media with useful alt text or adjacent text equivalents.

The implementation can launch using the semantic compositions alone. Optional raster layers can replace labelled placeholders after prompt generation and review.

Optional ranking-shift raster brief: 1600×1000, safe 4:3 center crop, pale paper background, three precise signal paths changing order near one release marker, teal and violet only, no text, no logos, no people, no generic charts, no glassmorphism, and no implied causation.

Optional shared-test raster brief: 1600×1000, safe 4:3 center crop, one central activation signal branching into two restrained paths, Product and Lifecycle, then reconnecting at a learning loop, existing palette only, no text, no logos, no email-envelope cliché, and no dashboard-card grid.

Alt-text intent for both: describe the relationship already stated in adjacent HTML, not visual decoration or invented data.

### Visual grammar and anti-template rules

Classification: **Hybrid landing page**. Landing-page rules govern the composition. App-UI rules govern the concept preview.

The approved preview grammar is an editorial instrument:

- One ranking ledger, not a dashboard mosaic.
- One selected signal, not several equal KPI tiles.
- One movement annotation, not decorative charts.
- One proposed test sequence, not a backlog or campaign builder.
- No sidebar, global app navigation, search, filters, saved views, notifications, or fake account controls.
- No generic icon feature grid, carousel, stock photography, decorative blobs, or purple gradient.
- The rounded dark surface exists because it is the interaction. Other sections remain mostly cardless.
- The hero background stays full-bleed paper. The instrument is not wrapped inside a second rounded hero container.

Motion has three jobs only:

1. Hero copy and the ranking ledger enter as one composed reveal.
2. Rank movement appears once when its explanation enters view.
3. Row selection glides the highlight and updates related text.

All three resolve immediately under reduced motion.

Litmus result for approved Variant A:

| Check | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Brand unmistakable in first screen | Yes |
| One strong visual anchor | Yes: signal-ranking ledger |
| Page understandable by scanning headlines | Yes |
| Each section has one job | Yes |
| Cards necessary | Yes only where the card is the interaction |
| Motion improves hierarchy | Yes, limited to the three jobs above |
| Premium without decorative shadows | Yes; offset tint may be removed without losing hierarchy |

No hard-rejection pattern is permitted in implementation.

### Design-system update

`DESIGN.md` must change with the homepage implementation:

- **Primary signature:** the evolving signal-ranking ledger. Rank, movement, descriptive lift, coverage, and next-test status form one inspectable object.
- **Supporting evidence motif:** treatment circles and control squares remain available only for experiment proof such as Typesy VIP.
- **Typography:** Source Serif 4 italic remains the expressive display face; Inter remains body and control text; JetBrains Mono remains instrument metadata.
- **Color:** reuse canvas, paper, ink, teal, teal tint, violet, violet tint, and hairline tokens. Add no hue.
- **Surface rule:** the dark instrument belongs only to decision-critical previews or evidence. Editorial explanation stays on the paper canvas.
- **Shadow rule:** one offset tint may separate the primary instrument. Secondary sections cannot repeat the same raised treatment.

New shared component vocabulary:

1. `Signal ledger`: ranked rows with one selected state.
2. `Movement annotation`: prior rank, current rank, and non-causal context note.
3. `Test lane`: Product, Lifecycle, or Combined explanation for the selected signal.
4. `Preview-state selector`: secondary disclosure for loading, empty, error, success, and partial concept states.

Existing buttons, focus rings, evidence boundaries, header, footer, typography, and spacing rhythm are reused.

### Interaction plan

- Signal selection changes the evidence explanation and ranking history.
- Product, Lifecycle, and Combined tabs change the intervention examples.
- Ranking movement animates once when the section enters view.
- Reduced-motion users receive the final state immediately.
- Every interaction has a semantic, visible non-JavaScript baseline.

### Interaction state contract

The concept preview deliberately demonstrates application states. Every state keeps this label visible: `Interactive concept preview using illustrative data. Not a live customer account.`

An optional `Inspect preview states` disclosure lets visitors switch states. It stays outside the primary conversion path.

| Feature | Loading | Empty | Error | Success | Partial |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Signal ranking | Three quiet skeleton rows and `Preparing the illustrative snapshot…` | `No signals ranked in this preview yet.` plus `See how the Sprint starts` | `The concept preview is unavailable.` plus an anchor to the Sprint method | Three canonical signal rows with the first selected | Available rows remain visible; unavailable values read `Review pending` |
| Selected-signal detail | Reserved text lines with no flashing animation | `Choose a signal to inspect its movement and proposed tests.` | Default signal explanation remains visible as plain text | Prior rank, observation, Product test, Lifecycle test, and sequence update together | Missing test copy reads `Not defined in this preview` |
| Product/Lifecycle views | Selected signal remains visible while view labels use a subtle progress treatment | Both lanes display the selected signal and `No test proposed for this view` | All three lane descriptions remain expanded in document order | Tabs expose Product, Lifecycle, and Combined content with selected state | Available lane stays active; unavailable lane is disabled with an adjacent reason |
| Ranking-shift media | Semantic timeline renders before optional art | Timeline displays the two snapshots without decorative media | Adjacent text explains the ranking movement | Release marker and two snapshots appear with the non-causal note | Missing optional art does not remove labels or values |
| External booking action | Button remains stable; no simulated spinner | Not applicable | Browser or Calendly failure stays external; the page never claims success | The ordinary external link opens in a new tab | Attribution failure does not block navigation |

State styling rules:

- Skeletons use the existing hairline and muted tokens. No shimmer or looping motion.
- Empty and error states use plain language, one useful next action, and no alarming red treatment.
- Disabled controls remain legible and include a visible reason.
- Simulated states never trigger analytics events named like real customer-data operations.

### Responsive and accessibility specification

#### Desktop · 1080px and above

- Hero uses the approved Variant A split: approximately 42% copy and 58% signal ledger.
- Headline, supporting sentence, CTA group, and preview read as one composition.
- The shared test map stays horizontal because both lanes remain visible at once.
- Reading sections use the established 680px editorial width where comparison is unnecessary.

#### Tablet · 761px to 1079px

- Hero copy sits above the full-width signal ledger before either becomes cramped.
- Ranking rows keep the table structure and all fields.
- The shared test map compresses its paths but retains the central signal and both lanes.
- Navigation keeps the existing two-row structure and scrollable route rail.

#### Mobile · 760px and below

- Order: eyebrow, headline, supporting sentence, primary CTA, secondary anchor, signal ledger.
- Each signal becomes one stacked ledger row. The top line contains rank, signal name, and movement. The second contains lift, coverage, and test status.
- All three signals remain visible. There is no horizontal document scroll and no hidden ranking context.
- Selecting a signal expands its prior rank, observation, and test sequence directly below that row.
- Product, Lifecycle, and Combined controls use a full-width segmented tab row with visible labels.
- The shared test map becomes a vertical sequence: approved data sample → selected signal → Product test → Lifecycle test → sequence note.
- The Typesy summary uses Treatment and Control side by side, followed by a full-width lift row.
- `Inspect preview states` remains a secondary `details` disclosure below the successful state.

#### Interaction accessibility

- Signal names are real buttons inside a semantic ranking table or ledger. Each uses `aria-selected` and `aria-controls` for its detail panel.
- Arrow keys move between signals; Home and End select the first and last signal. Tab leaves the ledger normally.
- Product, Lifecycle, and Combined use the existing roving-tab pattern with visible focus and selected state.
- The dashboard anchor moves focus to the dashboard heading after scrolling. Reduced-motion users receive instant movement.
- Updated detail content does not steal focus. Its heading and selected control expose the relationship.
- Every control is at least 44px in both dimensions at touch viewports.
- Body text remains at least 16px with 4.5:1 contrast. Instrument metadata may be smaller only when non-essential and still legible.
- Movement never relies on color alone; arrows and text labels accompany teal or violet.
- Optional images have meaningful alt text or empty alt when adjacent HTML already conveys the relationship.
- At 200% zoom, content reflows without clipping, overlap, or two-dimensional scrolling.

### Language rules

- Prefer: activation signal, leading indicator, ranked hypothesis, product test, lifecycle test.
- Avoid: PAI, PP, lifecycle rule, behavioral state, intervention unless defined.
- Explain technical concepts through outcomes before metrics.
- Use short paragraphs and visual grouping, not arbitrary twenty-word deletion.
- Preserve useful detail when it answers trust, security, method, or scope questions.
