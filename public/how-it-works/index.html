Five working steps

A short path from hypothesis to readout.

Each step tests part of the theory without treating correlation as proof.

  1. Frame the hypothesis.

    Name the candidate behavior, eligible cohort, outcome, and observation window.

  2. Audit the candidate signal.

    Check event quality, coverage, timing, and its baseline relationship with the outcome.

  3. Design the intervention and holdout.

    Predefine treatment, control, exposure, primary outcome, and stop rules.

  4. Measure behavior and outcome.

    Determine whether the intervention moved the candidate and whether the outcome changed between arms.

  5. Deliver the readout.

    Report both results, the uncertainty, and a go, stop, or iterate conclusion.

Evidence guardrails

Every claim keeps its boundary.

Correlation

Shows an association. It does not prove the behavior caused the outcome.

Leading indicator

Shows whether the candidate behavior moved. It does not establish that the outcome will follow.

Controlled lift

Compares treatment and control on the predefined outcome. See the corrected Typesy case study, where the behavior moved and conversion did not.

Operating rule

Earns a go, stop, or iterate conclusion only from the controlled readout.
